# Causal response: anticipation and impact

**Trigger.** Match when an action, change, or event should visibly cause a response, consequence, or state update.

**Compositional purpose.** Keep the control or causal actor legible, show its meaningful action, then make the target respond as a visibly linked consequence. Where the cause needs emphasis, allow a brief anticipation before impact; let the target's response and resulting state settle for inspection. A live control/target pair may stay in place and update together, while a collision or replacement may use linked displacement. Emphasis can come from timing, contrast, or a pause, not only added movement.

**Failure checks.** Check that the cause precedes its effect; the effect appears on the semantic evidence object; anticipation does not imply unsupported intent; and impact is not replaced by unrelated decoration or an unexplained jump cut.
